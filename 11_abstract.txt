Medicinal-plant traceability requires links between environmental observations, material lineage, and the people responsible for cultivation and downstream medicinal-use records. We present an edge Internet of Things (IoT) architecture combining local sensing, deterministic monitoring, digital supervision, QR-based passports, and human-machine interfaces. PLANT2CLINIC provides the reference implementation: MQTT telemetry and SQLite persistence support room-state monitoring, while local traceability records and human-reviewed dossiers preserve production context. A production replica supports inspection of copied operational state. Complementary mobile demonstrators address farmer activities and healthcare research workflows; their integration boundaries and simulated inputs are stated explicitly. Local AI extends evidence retrieval and explanation without determining alarm states. Evaluation combines an audit of 55,266 stored field-monitoring evaluations, robustness tests, audit workflows, and separate evidence-assistance benchmarks. The consistency audit found no discrepancies in 136,005 recorded anomaly-band decisions or 54,982 aggregated room states, excluding 284 freshness states; it does not independently validate anomaly detection or agronomic thresholds. A separate 100-case timing and robustness probe reported a median evaluation time of 0.24 ms, 96 controlled responses, and four input-validation exceptions. Seven audit workflows completed. The mobile interfaces illustrate complementary interactions but were not subjected to user studies or an end-to-end interoperability trial. The architecture supports a plant-to-care information pathway with human oversight and bounded AI assistance. Clinical demonstrations use fictitious data; clinical effectiveness and predictive digital-twin behaviour remain outside the validated scope.
